Executive Summary
Pakistan is home to one of the largest youth populations in the world, with over 22 million high
school students. Yet structured academic and career guidance is almost non-existent. Most
schools lack counselling resources, private consultants are prohibitively expensive, and
students are left to make life-changing academic decisions based on guesswork, hearsay, or
social pressure.
Our solution is an academic pathway app that democratizes access to guidance. Students
begin with free assessments that identify their academic strengths, interests, and values. Based
on these insights, the app then offers premium features: curated lists of local and international
universities, step-by-step admissions roadmaps, and access to a marketplace of vetted tutors
and mentors.
By launching first in Pakistan, we capture a massive underserved market and create a scalable
model that can later be expanded.
The Problem in Pakistan
● No Access to Guidance: The majority of schools lack dedicated career counselling.
● Expensive Alternatives: Elite consultants charge upwards of PKR 200,000 ($700+) per
student, out of reach for most families.
● Information Asymmetry: Students and parents have little credible information on
university options, scholarships, or future career paths.
● Cultural Narrowness: Career aspirations are typically limited to “doctor, engineer,
lawyer,” ignoring emerging fields like data science, renewable energy, and design.

Result: Students make uninformed choices, often leading to misaligned degrees, wasted
resources, and reduced employability.

The Solution
Our app fills this gap by providing an accessible, engaging, and personalised guidance
platform.

Free Core Offering
● Fun, interactive assessments in English and Urdu.
● Reports showing top academic fields, learning styles, and career matches.
● Instant clarity for students: “These are your strengths, and here’s where they could take
you.”

Premium Pathway Features
● Dream University Lists: Tailored recommendations of Pakistani and international
universities.
● Admissions Roadmaps: Clear requirements, deadlines, and scholarship options.
● Tutors & Mentors Marketplace: Verified tutors for SAT, MDCAT, ECAT, IELTS, and
essay prep.
● School Dashboards: Premium accounts for schools to guide their student cohorts.

Assessment Flow – Student Journey
Step 1: Onboarding (5 mins)
● Quick profile setup: age, grade, subjects taken.
● Ask: “What do you hope to do after school?” (doctor, engineer, unsure, etc.)
● This sets a baseline of current awareness.

Step 2: Interest Mapping (10 mins)
● Tool: Shortened Holland RIASEC Test (20–30 questions, gamified).
● Outcome: Identifies career interest type (e.g., Investigative → sciences, Social →
teaching/psychology, Artistic → design/media).
● Output: “You’re strongest in [Investigative + Social]. Fields to explore: medicine, data
science, teaching, research.”

Step 3: Strengths & Personality Snapshot (10–12 mins)
● Tool: Mini Big Five Personality + custom academic strengths quiz.
● Outcome: Shows learning style & strengths.
○ Example output: “You thrive in structured environments, prefer independent work,
and have strong verbal reasoning.”

● Actionable link: Suggests study styles + careers aligned with strengths.

Step 4: Skills Snapshot (5–7 mins)
● Tool: Cognitive skills mini-test (logic, language, pattern recognition).
● Outcome: Maps relative academic strengths → math-heavy vs. verbal vs. creative.
● Actionable link: Recommends majors/programs.
○ e.g., “Your logical reasoning is strong → fields like Computer Science,
Engineering, Finance may be a great fit.”

Step 5: Motivation & Values (5 mins)
● Tool: Custom reflection quiz (“Would you prefer security or innovation?”).
● Outcome: Surfaces student priorities (money vs. passion, stability vs. exploration).
● Actionable link: Matches priorities to fields + lifestyles.

Step 6: Career Awareness Boost (Pakistan Add-On) (Optional)
● Tool: Quiz that introduces “hidden careers.”
● Outcome: Broadens horizons beyond “doctor/engineer.”
● Actionable link: Suggests emerging fields (AI, data, green energy, design, policy).

Test 1: Holland RIASEC Test
The Holland Code, also known as Holland Occupational Themes (RIASEC), is a theory of
career choice and vocational interests developed by John L. Holland, an American psychologist,
in the late 1950s. The theory suggests that people's career choices are influenced by their
personality types and that certain personality types are more likely to be attracted to specific
career fields.

Code

Step 1: Interest Layer (RIASEC)
The student takes the RIASEC test.
They get a Top 2 interest types, e.g., Investigative + Artistic.
This tells us: “These are the types of careers you naturally enjoy.”
Example:
Investigative + Artistic → Research, Design, Data Science, Media
Think of it as the first filter: only keep careers that match their natural interests.
Step 2: Values Layer
The student takes the Values test.
You check what drives them: Stability? Creativity? Growth? Money? Impact?
Filter and rank careers from Step 1 based on these values.
Example:
Student is high on Creativity and Growth → push design, research, STEM
Student is high on Stability and Impact → push teaching, accounting, law
This is the second filter: “Careers that match what matters most to you.”
Step 3: Personality Layer
Student takes Mini Big Five (personality) test.
Each career has a “personality fingerprint” — what traits usually succeed in that career.
Compare student traits → score a match percentage.
Example:
CS → needs high openness + conscientiousness
Design → high openness + extraversion
Teaching → high agreeableness + social skills
This is the final filter / fine-tuning: “Of the careers you like and that match your values, which
ones suit your personality best?”
Step 4: Combine Scores and Rank

Take the interest match, values match, and personality match.
Give each a weight (e.g., Interest 50%, Values 30%, Personality 20%).
Multiply scores, sum them → final score.
Sort careers by final score → top 5–10 careers = student’s recommended paths.

Manzil — Student Direction & Career Clarity Program
A structured guidance pilot for senior students

The Problem Schools Face
Many capable students struggle to choose subjects and majors that truly fit them.
Guidance often happens late, under pressure, and relies heavily on subjective advice.
This leads to:
• Students changing majors or losing motivation
• Parent anxiety and dissatisfaction
• Counselors managing repeated crises instead of proactive guidance
Schools are increasingly expected to support students not just academically, but directionally.
What Manzil Is
Manzil is a web-based student guidance tool that helps learners understand:
• What they naturally enjoy (interests)
• What motivates their choices (values)
• How they operate best (personality)
Using well-established psychological frameworks (RIASEC, Schwartz Values, Big Five), Manzil
provides clear, age-appropriate guidance in language students understand.
It does not replace counseling, it supports it.

What Makes Manzil Different?
• Structured, not subjective
Results are grounded in validated models, not guesswork.
• Student-friendly
Explanations are designed for teenagers, not psychologists.
• Live & interactive
Students see insights emerge as they complete the assessment.
• Transparent for parents
Optional parent-view explains the logic behind recommendations.

Value for the School
1. Reduces Risk of Poor Subject & Major Choices

Students receive clarity earlier, lowering the chance of misalignment later.
2. Supports Counsellors
Counselors begin conversations with a structured student profile, saving time and improving
quality.
3. Strengthens School Reputation
Parents see the school investing in thoughtful, evidence-based guidance.
4. Creates Early Alignment
Guidance happens before application stress and last-minute decisions.
What Students Gain
• Clear understanding of their strengths and preferences
• Confidence in academic direction
• Reduced comparison anxiety
• Language to explain their choices to parents and teachers
Students leave calmer, not more confused.
